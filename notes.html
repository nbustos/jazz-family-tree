Notes on Notes

How jazz composition changed through the ages — and the five threads that never broke. Thirty pieces, three from each era, read closely for their form, harmony, rhythm and the balance between what was written and what was made up on the spot.

The staff

Each line is a throughline. Each piece is a chord of note-heads, stacked on every line it sounds. Hover to see a piece; click to read it. Click a line's name to follow that thread.

Sources & method

Every finding on this page is drawn from published musical analysis — jazz history textbooks, monographs, journal articles and the composers' own accounts — and each is footnoted to its source on the piece where it appears. Musical facts (keys, forms, bar counts, recording data) were cross-checked against at least two sources; where sources disagree, the text says so. Melodies of works still under copyright are described in words rather than notated. The short “hear it” sketches are synthesized in your browser and illustrate harmony or rhythm only.
