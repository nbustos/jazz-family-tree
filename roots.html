Deep Roots

Jazz grew from roots that run back across the Atlantic to a continent of more than a thousand musical traditions. This is a journey through them: the kingdoms and peoples, the instruments and the principles of their music, the social worlds music lived in — and how, through the catastrophe of the slave trade, those principles crossed the ocean and became the soil of a new music.

Sources & method

Each region and section was researched from published scholarship in ethnomusicology, history and sociology, and cross-checked against at least two sources; footnote numbers link to the sources for that section. Where scholars disagree — about how much of African practice survived the crossing, or about the origins of a particular rhythm or instrument — the text says so and names the positions. Photographs are from Wikimedia Commons under public-domain or Creative Commons licenses, credited beneath each image. Embarkation figures are the estimates of the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database (SlaveVoyages). The map is drawn from Natural Earth (public domain); modern borders only approximate historical regions.
