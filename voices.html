Conjoining Voices

Every instrument in a jazz band is a different way of setting air in motion — buzzing lips, a beating reed, a struck string, a ringing bar, a voice. Here you can hear how each one makes a note, see where it lives in the spectrum of human hearing, and listen to what happens when the voices join: chords, grooves and stories.

Sound on. Everything here is played from real recordings of real instruments.

Part one

The hearing spectrum

Each bar is an instrument's range of fundamental pitches; the faint line beyond it is how far its overtones reach. The horizontal axis is frequency on a logarithmic scale, so every octave — and every piano key — takes the same width. Click an instrument to hear its low, middle and high register and watch its harmonics light up.

Sources, samples & method

Facts about how instruments work, their ranges and their spectra come from musical acoustics references; statements about emotion distinguish empirical findings from historical and critical observation. Voicings are taken from published scores, analyses and arranging textbooks, as cited on each. Harmonic fingerprints and envelopes are measured live in your browser from the same recordings you hear. Notes between recorded pitches are produced by resampling the nearest recording by up to a semitone or so.
