Playlists

A listening list for every era on the tree, beginning before it with the African and diaspora music traced in Deep Roots. Each playlist appears three times, once for each streaming service, so you can listen wherever you already do. Track lists are the same in every column; only the links change.

About these lists

The tracks were chosen to give the sound of each era and its main branches on the tree: landmark records, the people the tree follows, and a few surprises. Years are when the track was recorded. Where the artist is on the tree, the liner notes link to them.

How the links work. Apple Music links go straight to the track when Apple’s public catalog search found the original recording: the title and artist agree, and the matches were checked by hand to weed out later re-recordings, live versions, edits and alternate takes. Where Apple only has another version, the link opens a search instead. YouTube and Spotify links open a search for the title and artist on that service, since neither offers a catalog search without signing in, and versions of old records come and go there. A magnifier marks a search link; an arrow (↗) marks a direct one.

Making your own copy. “Copy list” puts the playlist on your clipboard as plain “Title — Artist” lines, the format that playlist-transfer tools such as Soundiiz and TuneMyMusic accept for building a real playlist in your account. Recordings made in the United States before 1926, like many in the first two lists, are in the public domain; several also play on this site’s own player, on the tree and on Labels & Locals.
