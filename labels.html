Labels & Locals

Jazz was made on bandstands and kept on records. This page follows both: the record companies, from Victor’s first “jass” disc to today’s musician-run labels, and the clubs, ballrooms, halls and lofts where the music was played, learned and argued over. Who owned them, who was paid, who was let in, and what each did to the sound.

    Sources & method

    Each label, venue, city and essay was researched from published histories, discographies, oral histories, obituaries and archival reporting, cross-checked against a second source where one exists. Footnote numbers link to the sources for that entry. Where accounts disagree about a date or a “first”, the text says so. Years are approximate where marked “c.”; a venue’s timeline bar runs from its opening to its closing as a jazz room, with gaps where it closed and reopened. Photographs are from Wikimedia Commons under public-domain or Creative Commons licenses and are credited beneath each image. Label logos and album covers are not shown, because they are not freely licensed. The world map is drawn from Natural Earth (public domain) in the Equal Earth projection. The 78s you can play are United States recordings published before 1926 and now in the public domain.
