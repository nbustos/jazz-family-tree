    Jazz Family Tree

    About the Jazz Family Tree

    This tree charts musicians and bands across branches and eras. Below ground, the roots trace the African and African American traditions — polyrhythm, call and response, the ring shout, spirituals and work songs — that met European harmony and Caribbean rhythm in New Orleans. Above, each era of the trunk sprouts branches for the styles and regional scenes that grew from it, and lineage lines connect musicians to their mentors, bandleaders and acknowledged influences.

    The layout is a simplification: musicians appear once, on the branch where they first made their mark, and list the other styles they played. Placement along a branch follows the year an artist first made an impact. Faint green offshoots mark kindred genres — gospel, R&B, soul, funk, hip-hop and others — that share jazz's roots.

    How it was researched

    Dates, places, key recordings and documented relationships were researched and cross-checked against Wikipedia, Britannica, AllMusic, the Oxford/Grove reference works, NPR, Jazz at Lincoln Center, label archives and published obituaries. Influence links are limited to documented relationships: teachers, bandleaders a musician apprenticed under, and influences the musicians themselves acknowledged. Corrections are welcome — open an issue on GitHub.
